Now we are going to compare two
powerful brands from completely
different industries, Netflix and Nika.
While one dominates global streaming
and the other leads India's beauty and
lifestyle market, both companies offer
valuable lessons in understanding
consumer lifetime value. First, let's
consider Netflix, which follows a
subscription-based model. In this model,
the average value of each sales is
fixed because the consumer pays a set
monthly subscription fee. As a result,
Netflix LTV, or the consumer lifetime
value, is relatively predictable.
Customer lifetime value is calculated
by multiplying the monthly subscription
fee by the number of months the
consumer remains subscribed. This means
the longer a consumer remains
subscribed, the higher their lifetime
value becomes. Next, let's look at NICA,
which operates on a transactional or
retail model. Unlike Netflix, consumers
are not limited to a fixed monthly fee.
They purchase different products in
different quantities at different
points of time. Because of this, there
is no clear ceiling on how much a
consumer can spend. Consumer lifetime
value is calculated in this case by
multiplying the average order value by
the number of orders a consumer places
in a particular year, month, or a
quarter by the number of orders a
consumer places in a year, and then
multiplying that by the length of the
consumer relationship.
Hence, customer lifetime value grows
based on how much customer spends per
order, how often they buy, and how long
they remain as customers. We can better
understand these models by examining
Netflix and Nyika. Across several key
points. First, consider revenue growth.
For Netflix, growth is primarily driven
by retention. The longer consumers stay
subscribed, the more revenue they
generate.
In contrast, Nike's growth is driven by
the basket size and the purchase
frequency, meaning how much consumers
buy and how often they return to shop.
Next, let's look at primary metric for
each business. For Netflix, the most
critical metric is churn rate, which
measures how many consumers cancel
their subscriptions. For Nyika, the key
metric would be the average order value,
or AOV, which reflects how much
consumers typically spend in each
purchase. The marketing goals also
differ. Netflix focuses on keeping the
service essential, encouraging
consumers to develop a habit of
watching the content regularly, while
Nyika, on the other hand, focuses on
creating discovery and desire,
motivating consumers to explore
products and continue shopping. Each
model faces a different risk factor.
For example, Netflix, the biggest
challenge is content fatigue, where
consumers may cancel if there is
nothing new or interesting to watch.
For Nika, the risk comes from
competition, price sensitivity, where
consumers might switch platforms such
as Amazon in search of better prices or
deals. So when analysing LTV, Always
consider how the company generates
revenue and keeps its customers engaged
over a period of time.
