Hello again. In this video, let's look
at how businesses adapt their sales
approach based on different situations
in different market conditions. We
would require different selling
patterns. Let's briefly understand two
distinct sales tactics. One is known as
waterfall tactic, and the other is
known as sprinkler tactic, and we'll
try to understand when to use each of
those. Waterfall technique means
selling in a step by step manner,
starting with the most important
consumer first and then moving downward.
This approach is suitable when the
consumer base is heterogeneous, meaning
consumers differ significantly in value.
It is also useful when product
perception is uncertain or not extreme.
The key action here is to separate high
value consumers from low value
consumers and prioritise them
systematically. Sprinkler technique
refers to selling many customer
segments at the same time, spreading
efforts widely. This technique is used
when you need to target. Multiple
consumer segments at once to quickly
gain market share. It works best when
the market is highly competitive and
speed is crucial and the product has
mass appeal. So essentially waterfall
is focused and sequential where
sprinkler is broad and rapid. With that
foundation in mind, let's examine how
they differ operationally. Starting
with risk level, waterfall technique
involves low and controlled risk
because the rollout is gradual. In
contrast, sprinkler carries higher risk
and higher cost since resources are
deployed at scale from the beginning.
In terms of speed, waterfall is slow
and steady. Allowing learning at each
stage, sprinkler is fast and aggressive,
aiming for immediate impact. Looking at
customization, waterfall allows high
customization, targeting one segment at
a time. Sprinkler relies on mass
message, so customization is relatively
low. The key resource also differs.
Waterfall requires time and patience.
Sprinkler demands capital and
infrastructure to support rapid
expansion. For example, Microsoft may
adopt a waterfall approach, first
selling to Fortune 500 companies in the
US, learning from those implementations,
and then expanding to mid-sized firms
in Europe 6 months later. On the other
hand, a fashion brand like H&M or Zara
or maybe a mobile game company like
Candy Crush follow a sprinkler-based
approach. They launch globally on the
same day because the shelf life of the
trend is very short and the product is
the same for everyone. Remember,
ultimately the choice depends on your
market conditions and strategic
business priorities.
