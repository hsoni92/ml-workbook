In this video, we will learn about consumer behaviors. If you have ever wondered why people buy certain products, the answer is consumer behavior. Consumer behavior refers to the study of how individual groups or organizations select, purchase, use, and dispose goods, services, ideas, or experiences to satisfy their needs and desires. It involves understanding the decision-making process of consumers, both internal and external factors that influence their choices. To better understand this influences, let's take a look at the key factors that affect consumer behavior. Consumer behaviors are shaped by multiple influences working together. There are six major factors affecting consumer behavior. psychological, social, cultural, personal, economical, and technological. In this module, we will focus on social and cultural factors. Let's begin with social factors. Social factors play a significant role in shaping consumer behavior. Families forms the foundation by influencing early preferences and buying habits. Reference group and social networks also have a powerful impact. Often influenced by their peers and social circles. Additionally, roles and status influence buying behavior. Consumers often choose products that reflect their social position. For example, teenagers might buy specific brands of clothing to fit in their peers, while adults may join a gym or pick up a new hobby to align with their social circle. Social proofs such as positive reviews or endorsements from influential figures can also drive decisions as people tend to trust those they perceive as similar to themselves. Now, let's look at how Culture affects consumer behavior. Culture shapes values, beliefs, and overall buying habits. Subcultures defined by factors like ethnicity, religion, or geographic location or shared interest also impact consumer decisions through common beliefs and values. Social class also plays a role in purchasing behavior by influencing buying power, brand preferences, and consumption patterns. For example, in cultures that prioritize collectivism, consumers make es decisions that benefit their family or social group. In contrast, individualistic cultures may emphasize personal preferences and self-expression when making buying choices. So together, social and cultural factors shape overall consumer behavior and influence purchasing decisions."
