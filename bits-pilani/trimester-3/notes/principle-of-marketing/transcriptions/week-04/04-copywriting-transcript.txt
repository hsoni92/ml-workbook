Welcome back again. In the previous
lesson, we looked at the marketing mix
and how the 4 P's and 7 Ps shape what
we offer, how we price it, where it's
available, and how it's promoted. To
bring those decisions to life, now we
turn to copywriting. In this lesson, we
will look at what copywriting really is,
why it plays a critical role in
marketing. So what is copywriting?
Copywriting is the art and science of
writing persuasive words known as copy
that prompts the readers or the users
to take an immediate action such as
purchasing a product, clicking on a
link, or subscribing to a newsletter.
It is essential for marketing and
advertisements with a focus on driving
conversions through email campaigns,
web content, and even ads. So how is
copywriting different from content
writing? Let's walk through some of the
differences one by one. Copywriting is
focused on immediate action. Its
primary goal is to hook attention and
influence the reader to act upon.
Content writing on the comparison is
focused on information and education,
helping users build trust and stay
engaged. When it comes to tone,
copywriting uses persuasive, punchy,
and often urgent language. Content
writing follows a more educational
narrative and informative style. In
terms of length, copywriting is usually
short, appearing in headlines, ads,
landing pages, and charts. Content
writing is usually longer, more
detailed, commonly found in blogs,
white papers, and case studies.
So many of you might be wondering why
you should still consider learning
copywriting in the age of AI or LLMs
like Gemini or TraGBT. The answer is,
in a business world driven by
technology, words, text and speech
still shape how people think, feel and
act. As the English writer Kipling once
said, words are of course the most
powerful drug used by mankind. Not only
words infect, egotize, narcotize, but
they do paralyse, they enter into and
colour the minute cells of the brain.
Narratives have the power to change us.
This is precisely why understanding and
crafting the right words remains a
critical skill even in the age of AI
and why copywriting continues to matter.
OK, so if you're curious how to build
the right copywriting habits, step one
is read a lot. One of the strongest
ways to build better copywriting skills
is to read widely and regularly,
whether it's novels, blogs, magazines,
newspapers, or any format you enjoy.
Consistent reading exposes you to
different writing styles and voices.
Reading regularly is an essential step
towards improving your own writing.
Firstly, it will teach you how other
people form their sentences, build
their paragraphs. Also, if you read
often, you will learn how to use jokes,
metaphors, and how to play with
language through real world examples.
For beginners, a useful reference here
could be a book by this lady called
Anne Hadley. The book name is Everybody
Writes. It's a beautiful book, which
helps you understand how to write
effectively and persuasively. Now
another important step on building the
right copywriting habits is first we
have to understand whether our writing
should be formal or informal on that
particular instance. Think of it like
choosing a dress and putting on makeup
for an event or a party. You usually
know in advance whether it's a formal
party or a casual party, and that
influences how you show up, what kind
of dresses you choose, what kind of
makeup you decide to put on. In the
same way, keep your brand and your
audience in mind and choose a tone that
fits both while choosing the
copywriting styles, be it formal,
informal, or other ways. Once you
decide a style, be consistent because
switching between formal and informal
can confuse readers and dilute your
message at that particular instance.
Here are a few of the practical tips to
further strengthen your writing habits.
Step one, focus on writing clear
paragraphs, with each paragraph centred
around a single theme or a single idea
to keep the message easy to flow. Don't
put too many ideas in a single
paragraph. Think of only one idea per
paragraph or one idea per couple of
sentences.
Tactic or strategy number 2, write
shorter sentences whenever possible.
Shorter sentences improve the clarity
and help maintain reader attention.
Like if you write more longer sentences,
the attention span of a reader might
break.
Strategy number 3, make an effort to
limit difficult words. Using simple and
familiar language makes your writing
more readable and accessible by large
masses of the population. Strategy
number 4, another key technique is to
use transition words are also known as
signal words, words like most important
because, therefore, or besides that,
guide readers through the text and
signal what's coming next, improving
the flow and direction. Finally, you
can also spice up your text by using
creative techniques. Metaphors such as
cold as and ice make ideas more vivid.
Think of metaphors as a guiding and
directional cues to your writer.
Storytelling is equally effective using
the PPT model or the place, protagonist,
and time. Introducing a conflict in
between and ending with a clear
resolution or a conclusion makes your
writing more vivid. The inverted
pyramid model is another useful
approach which is widely used in
journalism. It involves placing the
most important information up front,
following the less important and the
least important details, so your core
message is communicated clearly.
Example, think of a journalist if she
has to report a fire in the
neighbourhood, so she will first start
with the most important information,
which is like. There has been a
ravishing fire at 5 a.m. in the morning
in the neighbourhood and 10 people got
injured, so that's the most important
information. The less important
information could be like the police
came a little later. There was a lot of
chaos in the neighbourhood, and finally
the least important information could
be, or the local politician or the MP
or the MLSS came much later and there
was further chaos when they came in. So
that's how you can use this tactic in
your copywriting strategies. Also,
don't forget to aim for variation, vary
sentence length structure to keep the
writing engaging and avoid monotony in
your writing style.
