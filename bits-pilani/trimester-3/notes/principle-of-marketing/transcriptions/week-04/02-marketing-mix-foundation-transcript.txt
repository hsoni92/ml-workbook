Let's understand the foundation of
marketing. At the core, the foundation
of marketing is putting the right
product in the right place at the right
price at the right time. It sounds
simple, right, but the real challenge
lies in doing it well, consistently at
scale and in a way that truly meets
consumer needs.
So to achieve this balance, marketeers
use a model called its marketing mix.
The marketing mix, often referred to as
the four Ps. It represents a set of
controllable elements or variables that
organisations use to influence and meet
the needs of their target consumers in
the most effective and efficient way
possible. These variables are grouped
into 4 key components, commonly called
as the four P's of marketing. The first
P stands for product. Then the 2nd for
price, the 3rd for place, and the 4th
for promotion.
Let's understand the marketing mix of
the four P's. First P stands for
product. Product refers to the features,
packaging, and services associated with
the product of that company that it
offers to its consumers. The second one
is the price. Price is nothing but the
cost of purchasing the product and
reflects the value being delivered. The
3rd is the place. Place refers to the
channels through which consumers can
buy the product, ensuring it is
available at the right location at the
right time. And finally, it's promotion.
Promotion covers the media and
communication channels a company uses
to promote and create awareness for its
product. Traditionally, the model was
built around the four P's of marketing
product, price, place, and promotion.
However, as marketing kept on evolving
and became more service oriented and
consumer focused, the strategy expanded.
Three additional elements were also
introduced people, process, and
physical evidence, forming what we now
refer to as the seven Ps of marketing.
