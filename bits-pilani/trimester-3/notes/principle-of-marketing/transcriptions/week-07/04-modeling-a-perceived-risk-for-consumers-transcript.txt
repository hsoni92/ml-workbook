Hello again. In this video we will
examine how risk perception shapes
consumer decision making. So what is
perceived risk? Perceived risk refers
to a consumer's personal sense of
uncertainty or potential negative
outcome when purchasing a product. This
risk tends to be higher for expensive
or luxury items such as cars, bags, or
electronics. As a result, buyers invest
more time and research, seeking
opinions from friends, experts and
trusted sources to minimise any
possible issues after the purchase.
Perceived risk, consumers often choose
established and reputable brands rather
than new or lesser known alternatives.
What are the types of perceived risk?
Perceived risk appears in several forms,
including functional, social, financial,
physical, time, and even psychological
risk. Understanding these categories
help marketeers tailor strategies that
build trust and reduce customer
hesitation. So what is the perceived
risk dimension and how it relates to
purchase intention? This framework
illustrates how different dimensions of
perceived risk influence a consumer's
purchase decision. Financial risk
reflects concerns about losing money if
the product does not perform as
expected. Psychological risk involves
worry about regret, emotional
dissatisfaction after the purchase.
Performance risk relates to the doubts
about whether the product will function
properly. Time risk represents the
possibility of wasting time if the
product. Repaired, replaced, or
returned. Social risk appears in two
forms concerns about retailer
reputation and concerns about social
approval or influence. All these
perceived risks come together and
directly affect how willing a consumer
is to make a purchase. Understanding
these dimensions helps marketeers
reduce uncertainty and strengthen
purchase intention. So how marketing
strategies can be used to lower the
perceived risk. This particular table
shows how marketers use different types
of perceived risk by aligning them with
the right psychological triggers and
communication strategies. For
functional risk, it's reduced by
demonstrating the proof of performance
through case studies, product trials,
and so on. For financial risk, it is
eased by offering refund policies that
eliminate the fear of loss. Social risk
is addressed through reviews and
testimonials that provide social proof.
Physical risk is minimised by
showcasing certifications that assure
safety and reliability. Time risk is
reduced by emphasising convenience
features such as simple setups or quick
onboarding. Finally, psychological risk
is managed by aligning the brand with
consumer's identity through mission
statements and corporate social
responsibility initiatives. Together,
all these strategies help marketeers
build trust, reduce uncertainty, and
increase consumer confidence in making
a purchase.
