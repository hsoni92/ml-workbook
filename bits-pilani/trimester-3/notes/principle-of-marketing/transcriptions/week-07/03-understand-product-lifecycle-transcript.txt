Hi again. In this video, we'll examine
the life cycle that most products
experience in the market. A product
life cycle tracks how a product
performs over time across 4 stages
introduction, growth, maturity, and
decline. In the introduction stage,
sales are low, and profits are often
negative due to high investment in
development, marketing, and
distribution. During the growth stage,
sales increase rapidly as market
acceptance improves, profits rise, and
investments begin to pay off. In the
maturity stage, sales peak, and so
profits. As the competition intensifies,
growth slows, and the focus shifts to
efficiency and differentiation. Finally,
in the decline stage, sales and profits
fall due to market saturation, changing
consumer needs, or new alternatives,
while investment recovery stabilises or
tapers off. Now let's talk about some
key strategies companies use on a very
pivotal stage, which is the
introduction stage. In the introduction
stage of a product's life cycle,
companies choose pricing and promotion
strategies that align with their market
goals. When both price and promotion
are high, the approach is called rapid
skimming, aimed at quickly recovering
the investment while building strong
awareness.
Whereas on the other side, a slow
skimming strategy uses a high price but
low promotional effort, focusing on
early adopters without heavy marketing
spend. On the other hand, rapid
penetration is a strategy which
combines a low price with a high
promotion to quickly capture market
share by encouraging faster adoption.
Finally, slow penetration uses low
price and low promotion, aiming for
steady, gradual market entry with
minimal risk and cost.
Remember, each strategy supports a
different objective depending on how
aggressively the company wants to
position its product in the market.
First, rapid skimming. Rapid skimming
is a strategy used during the
introduction stage when a large portion
of the market is still unaware of the
new product. The consumers who are
aware tend to be early adopters. They
are willing to pay a higher price
because competition is also expected to
enter soon. The company must build
strong brand preference quickly and
efficiently. This approach helps
recover initial investment while
establishing the brand before arrivals
catch up. Now moving on to the next
strategy, which is slow skimming. Slow
skimming is used when the overall
market is small and most potential
consumers already know about the
product. Because buyers are willing to
pay the price, the company can set a
high price without investing heavily in
promotion. Competition is not expected
immediately, so there is no urgency to
accelerate brand building. This
strategy allows the company to generate
profits gradually while keeping
marketing costs low. Next is rapid
penetration. Rapid penetration is used
when the market is large, but most
consumers are still unaware of the
product. Because the market is highly
price sensitive, companies set a low
initial price to encourage quick
adoption. Competition is expected to
become strong, so gaining market share
early is essential. As production
scales and experience builds, Unit
manufacturing costs began to fall,
making the strategy more profitable
over time. Rapid penetration helps
companies establish a strong presence
quickly while preparing for future
competition. Finally, slow penetration
strategy. It is used when the market is
large and buyers already have a high
awareness of the product category.
Because the market is price sensitive,
companies adopt a low price strategy to
attract consumers, but they do so with
limited promotional spending. There is
some competition, but not enough to
require aggressive marketing. In this
approach, the company benefits from
Pioneer Advantage, arriving early
enough to build a stable consumer base
while keeping costs under control. Slow
penetration also allows the brand to
grow steadily, without heavy investment
or pricing pressure.
