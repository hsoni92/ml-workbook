Welcome back. In the previous video, we
discussed responsible marketing and the
role companies play in protecting
consumer rights and environmental
sustainability. In this video, we'll
shift the discussion slightly and focus
on something more fundamental. We'll
look at the role of human needs in
marketing. Despite rapid changes in
technology, media platforms, and
consumer behaviour, the basic
motivations that drive human decisions
still remain the same. Understanding
these motivation is important for
marketeers because it explains why
certain messages resonate with people
while others do not. One of the most
widely discussed frameworks for
understanding human motivation is
Maslow's hierarchy of needs. This
framework suggests that human behaviour
is driven by a progression of needs. At
the base there is psychological needs
such as food, water, and shelter. Above
this come safety needs, which includes
financial security and physical
protection. The next level focuses on
belongingness, which reflects our
desire for relationships, community,
and social acceptance. After that comes
self-esteem, which relates to
recognition, confidence, and
achievement. At the highest level is
self actualization, where individuals
seek personal growth and fulfilment.
For marketeers, this framework provides
a useful lens for understanding what
motivates consumer behaviour.
Let us make this idea more practical.
Many brands design their marketing
strategies around those human needs
pyramid structures. Insurance companies
often appeal to safety by highlighting
protection and stability. Social
platforms and membership programmes
appeal to belongingness by creating
communities around shared interests.
Luxury brands frequently appeal to
esteem by signalling status,
recognition, or achievement, and some
brands connect with self-actualization.
By aligning themselves with the
purpose-driven goals of personal growth,
understanding this Maslow's hierarchy
of needs and mapping it with human
connections help marketers design
communication that resonates with real
motivations rather than superficial
trends or messages. To conclude this
lesson, remember that marketing works
best when it aligns with genuine human
needs. While advertising techniques and
media platforms will continue to evolve,
the fundamental motivations that drive
human decisions remain relatively
stable. When marketers understand these
motivations, they can build messages
that create meaningful connections to
consumers. At the same time, this
understanding also carries
responsibility. Marketing should aim to
support genuine needs rather than
exploit insecurity. Balancing
effectiveness with responsibility is an
essential art of ethical marketing
practise.
