follow-up question If marketing has the
power to influence behaviour and

with that influence? In this video,

Sustainability in marketing practise.
Understanding these ideas helps
marketeers balance business goals with
broader social impact. Let us begin
with the concept of consumer rights and
broader movements such as consumerism.
Consumerism is an organised effort
aimed at strengthening the power of
buyers in the marketplace. It argues
that customers should have the right to
be informed about the product quality,
safety, pricing, and business practises
even before making a purchase decision.
Traditionally, consumer rights focus
mainly on safety and performance. Today
the discussion has expanded. Many
consumer advocates now emphasise
transparency, ethical sourcing and
responsible communication from
companies. This shift reflects the
broader change in expectations.
Consumers are no longer passive
recipients of marketing messages. They
increasingly act as an active
stakeholder who expects businesses to
operate responsibly. Another important
dimension of responsible marketing is
environmental sustainability.
Environmentalism encourages companies
to think beyond short-term consumption
and. The long term impact of their
activities on the planet. Instead of
maximising consumption alone,
businesses are increasingly expected to
focus on improving the quality of life
while predicting environmental
resources. A key concept in this
decision is carbon footprint. This
refers to the total environmental
impact created by a product or service
across its entire life cycle.
It includes the sourcing of raw
materials, manufacturing,
transportation, usage, and even
disposal. Reducing this footprint
requires coordinated effort from both
the companies and consumers. To address
environmental challenges systematically,
many companies adopt a structured
sustainability framework. One commonly
used framework is the sustainability
grid. This framework distinguishes
between the different levels of
environmental responsibility. The first
level focuses on internal improvements,
such as reducing waste, improving
energy efficiency, and lowering
emissions within company operations.
The next level moves beyond internal
processes and focuses on product
stewardship. This means designing
products in ways that reduce
environmental impact throughout their
life cycle. The most advanced stage
involves integrating sustainability
into the company's overall strategic
vision. At this stage, environmental
responsibility is not treated as a
separate initiative, but becomes part
of the core business strategy. When
sustainability aligns with the business
performance, responsible marketing
becomes both practical and scalable. In
the next video, we'll go deeper and
look at something more fundamental, the
human motivations that underpin all of
this, because ethical marketing
ultimately starts with understanding
what people actually need.
