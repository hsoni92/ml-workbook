measures. The percentage of net sales
that translates into marketing

Before we calculate marketing ross, we
need to understand another concept
called as net marketing contribution.
Net marketing contribution represents
the profit attributable to marketing
after accounting for production costs
and marketing expenses. It is
calculated. By taking net sales and
subtracting the cost of goods sold and
marketing expenses, the result shows
the profit generated by
marketing-driven sales. Now that we
have understood what net marketing
contribution is, now we can go ahead
and calculate marketing return on sales.
The formula is simple. We divide net
marketing contribution by net sales.
The result shows the percentage of
revenue that becomes. Marketing driven
profit. For example, suppose marketing
activities generate ₹150,000 in net
sales and produce ₹10,000 in net
marketing contribution. In this case,
the marketing ross or return on sales
would be around 6.6%. This tells us
approximately 6.6% of the sales revenue
represents marketing generated profit.
Alongside marketing ross, managers
often use another metric. marketing
efficiency ratio or MER. While
marketing Ross focuses on profit
contribution, MER provides a broader
view of the marketing efficiency across
all channels. It is calculated by
dividing total revenue by total
advertising spend. Because it looks at
the entire marketing system rather than
individual campaigns. MER is useful in
situations, but precise attribution is
difficult. A healthy Air is often
considered to be between 3 and 4. This
means that every unit spent on
advertising generates roughly 3 to 4
units of revenue. Now let us connect
these metrics back to marketing
decisions. Marketing ross matters
because it directly links marketing
activity to profitability. It allows
managers to compare campaigns on the
basis of contribution to profit, not
revenue alone. For example, two
campaigns may generate similar sales
numbers. However, one campaign may
require much higher marketing
expenditure. Marketing Ross reveals
this difference. This insight allows
managers to allocate budgets more
efficiently and effectively. When
interpreting marketing ross, it is
useful to compare higher and lower
values over time. A higher marketing
ross usually indicates strong
contribution and efficient marketing
investment. It suggests that marketing
spend is generating profitable growth.
A lower marketing loss may signal that
marketing costs are too high relative
to revenue generated. However, context
is important. During brand building
phases, companies may accept lower
short-term returns in order to build
long-term awareness and demand.
