Hi and welcome back. In this video we
will begin looking at one of the most
practical questions in marketing, which
is how companies decide their
advertising budgets. So in this
conversation we will begin with two
important metrics that marketers use to
evaluate advertising investments. These
are customer acquisition cost and
customer lifetime value. Let us begin
with customer acquisition cost, often
called as CAC. Customer acquisition
cost represents the total cost required
to convince a potential customer to buy
our product or service. You can think
of it as a price tag attached to every
new customer who walks through the door.
This cost includes several elements,
such as advertising spend, marketing
tools, agency retainers, salaries to
the employees, related to sales and
marketing activities. Understanding CAC
helps companies evaluate how expensive
is it to grow their customer base. Now
that we understand what CAC represents,
let us see how it is actually
calculated. The formula is pretty
straightforward. We divide the total
sales and marketing expenses by the
number of new customers acquired during
the same period. It is important that
both the numbers come from the same
time period. If we measure monthly
expenses, we should also measure
customers acquired in that month. This
gives us the average cost of acquiring
one. At this stage you might notice
that CAC alone does not tell the full
story. Imagine a company spends ₹1000
to acquire a customer. At first it may
seem expensive, but suppose that
customer spends ₹10,000 with the
company over several years. In that
case, the acquisition cost is actually
justified. So instead of looking only
at the cost, marketers also need to
understand the value generated by a
customer over time. This brings us to
the next key metric, which is lifetime
value. Life value, commonly referred as
LTV, measures the total revenue a
customer is expected to generate during
their entire relationship with the
business. While CAC focuses on the cost
of acquiring customers, LTV focuses on
the long-term value of those customers
create. Companies aim to increase
lifetime value because it represents
the long-term return on marketing
investment. A higher LTV means the
business can justify acquisition cost
while still maintaining profitability.
Let us now see how LTV is calculated.
The calculation typically combines
three components. The first is the
average purchase value, which measures
how much a customer spends in a single
transaction. The second is purchase
frequency, which indicates how often
customers buy. The third is customer
lifespan, which reflects how long the
customer remains active. When these 3
numbers are multiplied together, they
provide an estimate of the total
revenue generated by a customer over
time. Because of this relationship,
many companies focus strongly on
increasing lifetime value. They may
work on improving retention so
customers stay longer. They may
encourage repeat purchases or offer
additional products through upselling.
When LTV increases, businesses become
less dependent on constant new customer
acquisition and can achieve more stable
growth.
