Hi again, welcome back. Now instead of
looking at the internal marketing
performance, we'll look at how a
company performs related to its
competitors. Let's first define what
market share actually means. Market
share refers to the percentage of total
industry sales captured by a particular
company during a given time period. It
provides a simple but powerful
indicator of competitive position. For
example, if the total market generates
₹100 crore in sales and one company
contributes to ₹10 crore. That
particular company holds almost a 10%
market share. When a firm's market
share increases, it generally indicates
the company is gaining strength
relative to its competitors. Now before
we begin calculating market share, it's
helpful to understand the different
ways it can be measured or calculated.
In practise, companies use two primary
models. One model focuses on revenue.
And the other focuses on financial
dominance of a company in a market. The
second model focuses on unit sold and
helps evaluate operational scale and
customer reach. Each model provides a
different perspective, and marketeers
choose the appropriate approach
depending on the objective of their
analysis. Usually revenue model is the
most preferred one. One common way to
measure market share is through the
revenue model, also known as the
value-based model. In this approach, we
divide the company's sales revenue by
the total revenue generated in the
industry and then multiply the result
by 100 to convert it into a percentage.
For instance, if a boutique generates
₹100,000 in revenue. While the total
market revenue is ₹1 million the
company's market share would be 10%.
This model is particularly useful in
industries where pricing differences
between competitors are significant.
The second approach measures market
position differently through volume
rather than revenue. Another related
concept is relative market share.
Instead of comparing a company with
entire industry, relative market share
compares the company's sales directly
with the sales of the largest
competitor. In this calculation model,
we divide the company's sales by the
sales of the market leader. If the
result is greater than one, the company
holds a leadership position. If the
result is less than, The company is
operating behind the leading competitor.
To conclude this lesson, market share
plays an important role in
understanding competitive performance.
Companies with higher market share
often benefit from stronger brand
recognition, economies of scale, and
greater pricing flexibility. However,
growth in market share should always be
balanced with profitability. When
market share metrics are analysed,
together with CAC, LTV and marketing
rulers, marketing managers gain a
complete understanding of both
financial performance and competitive
positioning.
