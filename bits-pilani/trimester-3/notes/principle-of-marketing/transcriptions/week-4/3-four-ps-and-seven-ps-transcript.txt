We have already covered the 1st 4
pieces starting with product. This is
what we offer to consumers, including
quality and value, branding and imagery,
user experience, key features, and
warranties that build trust. Second is
price. Price focuses on the value and
the cost of the product. This includes
pricing strategy, list price, discount
payment methods, free elements, credit
terms that influence purchase decisions.
Third is the place. Please address us
how and where customers can purchase
the product. This could be through a
company website, online marketplace
such as Amazon or eBay, retail
environments, international exports,
wholesale channels, or even resellers.
Finally, promotion explains how
consumers discover the product. This
includes messaging, search marketing,
social media advertisements, direct
marketing partnerships, offline
activities, word of mouth, or even
promotional campaigns. Now, as we have
learned already, beyond the 1st 4 P's,
now the model extends to another 3
elements or another 3 P's.
The first P, or the first extended P
stands for people. People looks at who
is hired by the organisation and how
they act, covering employees, founders,
consumer facing staffs, culture and
image, and even training. The second
extended P is process. Process focuses
on how products or services are
delivered consistently. This includes
standardisation, measurement delivery
methods, complaint handling, and
responses time, and even ongoing
research and development. And the final
extended P stands for physical evidence,
which refers to the environment
surrounding the. Customer experience.
This includes offices or stores,
packaging, equipment and facilities,
recommendations, cleanliness, word of
mouth, interior design, music or sounds,
smell, and staff appearance. OK, so we
have covered all the 7 Ps of marketing.
Together these 7 P's or 7 elements help
marketeers design experiences that are
consistent and consumer focused.
