Hi again. In this video we will explore
why price elasticity of demand matters
for marketeers. By now, as you've
learned, marketing is essentially the
art of making a product so desirable
that its demand becomes inelastic.
When consumers strongly prefer your
brand, price becomes secondary. This is
where cross price elasticity of demand
comes into play. It measures how the
demand for one product changes when the
price of another product changes. In
other words, if a competitors raises
their price, the marketers want to know
how that might change their effect in
their own sales. Understanding these
dynamics helps guide marketeers to
craft a great marketing strategy. For
example, when the goal is to increase
market share in markets with high
elasticity, companies often use
aggressive price promotions. When the
demand is less elastic and the
objective is to increase profit margins.
Brands may raise prices and focus on
premium or luxury positioning. For a
new product launch where elasticity is
still uncertain, companies may use
price skimming to identify the maximum
price consumers are willing to pay. And
when defending against rivals or
substitute products, the key strategy
is product differentiation, which
reduces direct price comparisons and
lowers price sensitivity.
