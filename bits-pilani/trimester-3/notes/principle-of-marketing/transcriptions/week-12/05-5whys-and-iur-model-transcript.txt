at an important reminder that sits
underneath all of them. This is a
concept called as the map is not the
territory. The map is not the territory.
This idea comes from a mathematician
Alfred published on a paper, The
Relationship of Mathematical model to
Physical Reality. It explained that any
model, framework, or representation is
just a simplified version of reality.
In simple words, the model is not the
reality. Sure, models help us to think,
but they always lease something out.
This matters because when we forget
this, we start trusting models blindly.
For example, you might ask a
model-based question, Should I invest
in Tesla? The data, the charts, the
revenue projections may look convincing,
but real life often behaves differently
from models. History gives us many
reminders. One of the key examples is
In the 1980s, Delights of America was
once described as the next Taco Bell
that completely failed despite
promising forecasts. The playbook
looked right, but the reality didn't
follow it. So how do we reduce the
model ladder? One powerful technique is
the five ways, developed by Sakichi,
the founder of Toyota. The idea is
simple. When a problem occurs, ask why
5 times to reach the root cause. For
example, you run through a red light,
you ask yourself why. The answer could
be you were late. Then again you ask
yourself why. The answer could be you
woke up late. You again ask yourself
why. The answer could be the alarm
didn't simply work. Then again you ask
yourself why. The answer could be the
battery was dead. Then you again ask
yourself why. The answer could be you
forget to check it. So the real problem
wasn't traffic, it was a habit failure.
Now let's look at IUR model. It
explains the distinction between
ignorance, uncertainty, and risk.
Ignorance is when both outcomes and
probabilities are unknown. Uncertainty
is when outcomes are somewhat known,
but the probabilities are unclear. Risk
is when probabilities are known and
measurable. A simple example is buying
a car. Buying a car from a stranger
without inspection is ignorance. Seeing
it online without car history is
uncertainty. Running a detailed report
and inspection turns it into risk,
something you can reason about. The
goal of good thinking isn't just to
eliminate risk, but to move forward
from ignorance. And uncertainty to a
calculated risk. If you're curious and
you want to go deeper into these ideas,
I would highly recommend these two
books. One is this book by Mr. Peter
called Against the Gods. It talks about
various mathematical models and
probabilities. And then another book by
a famous statistician, Nasim Talib. The
book name is Fooled by Randomness.
