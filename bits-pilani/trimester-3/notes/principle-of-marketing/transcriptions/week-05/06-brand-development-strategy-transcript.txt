This framework presents four options. First, line extension. It works when you have the same category but with new formats. For example, when Coca-Cola launched Diet Coke or Cherry Coke or Zero Coke. Brand extension, it talks about when you have the same brand name but a new category. For example, when Dove launched Dove Men Plus Care or Dove Men Plus shampoos. Mult Brand strategy launching a completely different brand within the same category. For example, Uni Liver launching multiple shampoo brands to capture more shelf space. New brand strategy creating an entirely new brand in a new category. This is essentially most of the companies start with.
Now let's understand how companies choose the rice strategy. In a line extension, both the brand name and the category remain the same. This strategy is relatively low cost because it leverages exist ing packaging, brand recognition and consumer trust. It also helps keep the brand fresh. However, the risk is cannibalization where the new product may take sales away from the original product.
In a brand extension, the brand name remains the same. The product enters a new category. This provides instant credibility since consumers already trust the brand. It's also cheaper than launching a completely new brand. However, if the new product fails, it may damage or dilute the original brand. brand's reputation.
In a multibrand strategy, the company introduces a new brand within existing category. This allows companies to capture more shell space and multiple price segments, strengthening their market presence. However, it can also create internal competition between the company's own brands.
Finally, a new brand strategy involves launching a new brand in completely new category. This approach often offers a clean slate free from the associations of existing plans and works well for radical innovations or high-risk markets. However, it requires significant investment in building awareness, trust and market presence.
Each of these strategies offers a path for growth. But the right choice depends on factors such as brand strength, market position, and the level of risk a company is willing to take. That is how a 2x2 brand development grid helps companies and marketeers decide their path forward.
