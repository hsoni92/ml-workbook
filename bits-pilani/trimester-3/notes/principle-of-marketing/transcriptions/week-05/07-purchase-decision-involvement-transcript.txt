Now let's understand how consumers make purchase decisions. Consumers often engage in a routine or habitual behavior when making low involvement decisions. These are automatic purchase decisions based on limited or past information. An example could be You could always order a diet coke at lunch when you're engaging in routine response behavior. High involvement decisions and extended problem-solving decisions. High involvement decisions carry higher risk. If they fail, they are more complex and often have a higher price tag. Examples include purchasing a car, a house or an insurance policy. These items are not purchased frequently but they are important to the buyer. In these cases, buyer do not engage in a routine response behavior. As mentioned earlier, these decisions are not very automatic. Instead, they use extended problem solving mechanisms. This spend they spend more time comparing features, prices, guarantees before making a decision. So, how do consumers decide which approach to use? Let's look at some problem solving frameworks. Extended problem solving happens when there is a high risk or high uncertaintity. Consumers take time to gather information and carefully evaluate options. Limited problem solving on the other side is a common impulse buying situations where decisions are made quickly with less information. Routine problem solving occurs when brand loyalty is present. Consumers rely on previous experience and trust with that particular brand making buying decisions almost automatic. The level of involvement and perceived risk determines how much effort a consumer puts in in making a purchase decision.
